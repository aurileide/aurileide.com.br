Storyboarding design ideas

Ideation is a very important part of the design process to develop any kind of product (here, I consider a service also a product). In 2016, during a Coursera's Human-Centered Design course I attended, by professor Scott Kelman, there was an assignment focused on creating new ideas to solve problems.

There we talked about storyboards. A storyboard presents a scenario that takes a hypothetical user from setting to satisfaction. A setting may be a problem, need, or desire embedded in a specific situation and satisfaction can be an outcome achieved through a design that addresses the problem, the need, or desire.

Storyboards can be used to show what a design enables the user to accomplish without specifying a particular user interface. And a good storyboard begins by introducing the problem: what does the user seek to do? Then, the subsequent panels walk through what the user does. It introduces how the user begins using the system, any exploration that they do, and how the design helps the user accomplish their goal.

Drawing is very much used in the storyboarding process because it helps the designer focus on the idea more than on the visual outcome. Since I am not a very good drawer (even I try to), developing design ideas using illustrations to show them was a challenge. But I love a challenge!

The assignment

We were told to settle 3 design ideas that we would like to storyboard. The first step of the process should be to brainstorm a long list of design ideas and then pick our favorites from among them, selecting three.

For each idea, we should create 2 storyboards showing alternative tasks or users. This means that in total, we’ll create 6 unique storyboards.

Each storyboard should comprise 5-8 panels and fit on two A4 sheets of paper. Computer-drawn work was forbidden; storyboards must be hand-drawn. Illustration quality was not necessary. The goal was human-centered content. Also, the fine detailed drawing was not encouraged because that’s not the point of a storyboard.

Panel sequence of storyboard should demonstrate the major steps/elements that an interface would need to fulfill.

The process

Since it was supposed to be a storyboard, fine detailed drawings are not required, but it might be black enough to allow a good photo or scan images.

Then, thinking about that, a thick, black marker for the drawings was then chosen because the black lines are strong and very good to capture.

After some consideration, and listing some ideas, the three ones I choose were:

#1 IDEA Donations of objects.

#2 IDEA Birthday reminder.

#3 IDEA Fix it yourself.

The #1 idea: Donations of objects

Problem: Maria is a teacher who buys a lot of products but doesn't use everything. She would like to find people interested in the objects and make donations to gain space at home.

Alternative 1: An app that could find people interested in receiving donations, so they could pick them up at the agreed address, or maybe send them via postal address for free.

Alternative 2: It's the same idea, but with a different way of achieving the results. Instead of exchanging addresses, people placed an online advertisement and subscribed to receive the material for free.

The #2 idea: Birthday reminder

Problem: I tend to forget the birthdays of friends and important people. There just isn't room in my head for so much information.

Alternative 1: An app to remind me of my birthday, suggest gifts, stores and prices.

Alternative 2: In this other version, the app would remind me of special dates, not just birthdays. It would also suggest gifts to me by getting insights into the other person's interests and social comments they made. The stores, prices, and purchases would all be set up online and the gift would be mailed. In the end, I would get free coupons from the online store if I recommended it to other users.

The #3 idea: fix it yourself

Problem: Jose is a college student who loves making and fixing his own things because he needs to save money but spends a lot of time on the web looking for DIY instructions.

Alternative 1: An app that finds and suggests videos from the web to help José fix something broken. Like a dropped button on his shirt, for example.

Problem: Marcos is a manager who travels a lot and sometimes has to do small repairs.

Alternative 2: Find a repairman near you. This app would help travelers/non-travelers to find a place to fix a broken thing and suggest the closest and cheapest one. This app would help travelers/or not to find a place to fix a broken thing and suggests the closer and cheaper one.

LESSONS LEARNED

I had a lot of difficulties with drawing things. Maybe if there had been more time, maybe I would have done it better, with a written script of each step of the process.

Repeating the process would certainly bring more clarity to each idea.

The detachment from the details of the drawings was liberating. Thinking about the app's functionality, while thinking about how to explain it through an illustration, transformed my way of thinking about problems and solutions. It meant that the idea had to be simplified so that I could draw it myself.

With the graphic need to summarize my ideas, I got pretty caught up in each one. But hey! This is part of the storyboarding process, right?
